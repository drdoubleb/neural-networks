    A foundation model, in miniature

    pretrained on 100 nuclei without a single label · reused later for every question

    So far every question trained its own network from scratch on labelled cases. A foundation model is pretrained once, on many unlabelled images, to turn any image into a short code; the questions come later, each with a few labelled cases on top of the code. Nothing here rebuilds the nucleus. The miniature encoder plays a game with no labels in it, spot the same nucleus: ① take a nucleus and make two views of it (a flip or rotation, or the other lab’s scan); ② the encoder turns each view into 8 numbers; ③ among all the other views in the batch, it must find the partner by comparing the numbers; ④ the weights are nudged so the partner scores higher next time. To win, the code has to keep what makes a nucleus itself (its outline, size, texture) and ignore what a view changes (orientation, stain), and that is exactly what the questions later need. The 100 nuclei come from all three questions; the network is never told which. Once it has pretrained, go to 2 · Train and choose the input the code: a single layer on these 8 numbers, with as few labelled cases as you like.

    The game: spot the same nucleus

    one view of the last batch (or of the nucleus you pick below) against every other view of the batch · no label anywhere

    The encoder turns every view into 8 numbers and compares them by cosine; the vote is a softmax over the cosines at temperature 0.2. The answer is the nucleus’s other view, framed in orange. At the start the vote is spread thin over every candidate; pretraining is nothing but making the partner win this game, for every view of every batch. Watch the orange bar grow as it runs.

    What the code learns to keep, and to ignore

    the same nucleus in all 8 orientations from both labs, and the code of each view · at the start and now · then another nucleus

    Each strip is one view’s code, the 8 numbers as bars on one scale per row. At the start a nucleus’s 16 codes are no closer to each other than to another nucleus’s: a random encoder gives every view much the same code, so nothing is told apart. Pretraining makes a nucleus’s own codes agree (orientation and stain are ignored) while a different nucleus’s codes move away (its identity is kept). Untick the other lab’s scans above, reset, and the right-hand half no longer lines up.

    The right-hand chart is measured on the side, never fed back: after each epoch a single layer is trained on the frozen code with 20 labelled training cases of each question and scored on 40 nuclei it never saw (the question’s 20 test nuclei and the 20 held-out ones). Pretraining itself never sees a label, yet the code becomes worth more for every question. Size is readable even from a random code; contour irregularity is not, and that is what the pretraining earns.

    Slides: one label for twenty nuclei

    weak supervision · the attention learns where to look

    A slide carries a diagnosis; its cells do not. Here every slide is 20 nuclei and one label, no atypical cells or atypical cells present (2 to 4 atypical nuclei among bland ones, atypical in any of the four ways of the atypia question), and nobody labels the nuclei. Each nucleus becomes its code from the frozen foundation encoder; a small attention network gives every nucleus a score, a softmax over the slide turns the scores into weights that add up to one, the nuclei’s codes are averaged with those weights into one summary of the slide, and a single layer makes the call. The only teacher is the slide’s label, yet the attention learns which nuclei matter: tick reveal to see where it lands. Switch to a plain average and compare.

    Measurements

    Nucleus Net is a teaching toy: a small network with hand-written backpropagation — optional convolution, up to two dense hidden layers — trained in your browser on synthetic blood counts and nuclei. Keys: 1 2 3 4 5 switch stages, space trains or pauses, N classifies the next test case; during a walk-through space pauses and ← → step. Every nucleus comes as two scans, ours and another lab’s with a weaker stain, for the lab-difference and shortcut-learning points; the fourth stage, which appears with recipe ⑪, pretrains a miniature foundation model on 100 of them without labels, and the fifth, with recipe ⑬, trains attention over slides of twenty nuclei with one label each. Regenerate the cases with node tools/generate_cbc.js and node tools/generate_nuclei.js.
